Google Tasks

Reimagining functionality for everyday task management

Google Tasks is one of the most-used productivity tools in the world — and one of the easiest to outgrow. This project reimagined how everyday task management could feel calmer, clearer, and more aligned with how people actually work through their days.

I focused on reducing friction in the moments that matter: capturing a task before you forget it, seeing what needs attention now, and moving through your list without the guilt of an ever-growing backlog staring back at you.

Google Tasks — 2025

Problem

To-do apps became anxiety machines. Every unchecked box is a small accusation, and the more features an app added — projects, tags, priorities, calendars — the more the list itself became the work. People were managing their task manager instead of their tasks.

Approach

One thing, then the next. The redesigned experience answers a single question and hides the rest: the default view is what to do now. Everything else — the backlog, the someday, the maybe — stays one deliberate tap away, so the list can't guilt you from the home screen.

Outcome

Fewer lists, more finished. Constraining the interface to the present moment turned an anxiety surface into a focus tool — the backlog still existed, it just stopped being the first thing you saw.
