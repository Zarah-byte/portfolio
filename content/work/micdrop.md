## About the Project

Micdrop is a print magazine celebrating South Asian musicians, producers, and visual artists whose work sits at the intersection of tradition and contemporary sound. The project started from a simple observation: South Asian talent is everywhere in global music culture, but rarely centered on its own terms in editorial design.

I built the magazine as a system — type, grid, photography treatment, and cover language — that could carry issue to issue while giving each artist room to breathe. The goal was editorial confidence without spectacle: let the work lead, and design in service of voice.

## Problem

South Asian artists are often framed as crossover curiosities rather than primary subjects. Existing music media either exoticizes the culture or folds it into a generic global playlist aesthetic — neither approach gives artists the editorial weight they deserve.

## Approach

A visual language rooted in rhythm, not decoration. I used bold typographic hierarchy, high-contrast black-and-white photography, and a modular grid that could flex between long-form profiles and shorter cultural commentary. Every layout decision asked: does this feel like an artist being interviewed, or an artist being packaged?

## Outcome

A cohesive first issue that reads as a statement of intent — a publication with a point of view, not a mood board. Micdrop proved that editorial design can center South Asian artistry with the same rigor and restraint applied to any major music publication.
